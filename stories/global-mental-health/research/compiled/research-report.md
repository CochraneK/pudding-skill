# Research dossier — Global mental health

**Question:** How large is the global mental-disorder burden, and where does treatment capacity lag behind need?
**Audience:** general audience
**Status:** EDITORIAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Executive answer

The evidence is already strong enough to build the next version of the story without waiting for a massive IHME export. WHO's latest synthesis estimates 1.095 billion people living with mental disorders in 2021 (13.6% age-standardized prevalence), while GBD 2021 attributes about 155 million DALYs to mental disorders and says almost all of that burden is nonfatal disability. The system response is much thinner and highly unequal: Mental Health Atlas 2024 reports a global median of 2.1% of government health spending allocated to mental health and 13.5 specialized mental-health workers per 100,000 people, with only around 1.1–2.4 in low/lower-middle-income settings versus 67.2 in high-income settings. Treatment evidence shows the same gradient, but with important data limitations. The executable route is therefore to build country-level capacity visuals now from WHO GHO and Atlas country profiles, use published GBD estimates for the global burden framing, and treat an IHME country-level prevalence/YLD request as an optional enhancement rather than a blocker.

## Conflicts and non-comparable figures

- **Psychosis treatment/service coverage figures of 29% and about 40% should not be read as a trend.** — claims: psychosis-formal-care-fact-sheet-2020-basis, psychosis-service-coverage-atlas-2024. Keep both as separately scoped evidence. The 29% figure is a WHO fact-sheet synthesis citing Mental Health Atlas 2020; the ~40% Atlas 2024 figure is derived from only 22 countries with sufficient data and the report labels it indicative rather than definitive. Do not draw a 29→40 improvement line.

## Recommendations

- **Do not block version 1 on a giant IHME country export** — WHO 2025 already gives publishable global burden estimates, while WHO Atlas 2024 and GHO provide the strongest directly actionable evidence on financing, workforce and service capacity.
- **Make the main story burden → capacity mismatch, not a prevalence league table** — Modeled prevalence is sensitive to case definitions, source coverage and modeling assumptions. Ranking countries by prevalence alone invites a false 'mentally healthier country' interpretation.
- **Use WHO Atlas country profiles as the next extraction target** — They are public, standardized and directly aligned with the editorial question; a country-capacity map can be built without an IHME request.
- **Treat psychosis service coverage and MDD treatment coverage as qualified evidence** — The psychosis estimate is based on only 22 countries and the depression meta-regression has sparse evidence from low-resource settings. These figures show a care gap but should not be over-precise.
- **Keep counts, rates, budgets and workforce measures in separate visual grammars** — A billion-person count communicates scale; age-standardized prevalence supports comparison; budget share and workers per 100,000 describe capacity. Combining them into a single index would hide important semantics.

Caveats:
- GBD prevalence estimates are modeled estimates, not diagnosed-case registries or direct measures of subjective wellbeing.
- Country-level mental-health system data have nonresponse and differing reference years; missing must never be encoded as zero.
- Atlas medians describe responding countries and should not be presented as population-weighted global averages.
- Psychosis service coverage in Atlas 2024 is based on only 22 countries and is explicitly described as indicative rather than definitive.
- Treatment estimates from systematic reviews carry wide uncertainty intervals, especially in low-resource settings.
- Do not compare the WHO fact-sheet 29% psychosis-care figure directly with the Atlas 2024 ~40% figure as a time trend.

## Research boundary

This dossier is an evidence and acquisition instrument, not an oracle. Material claims still require editorial/domain review, and a secondary source should not replace an accessible primary source for a key number.
